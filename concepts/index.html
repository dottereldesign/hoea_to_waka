The Path Between Us

Two simplified people stand side by side, seen from behind at a slightly elevated angle. Their inward-facing silhouettes leave a tapered Paper-white opening that reads as a path continuing into the distance. Neither is pulling, rescuing or leading the other. Both face the same open space.

the relationship makes room for possibility.

Art direction

Colour deep teal and coral figures; a tiny peach sun ahead; white pathway.

Make it work the gap must deliberately read as a route. Keep it broad and avoid complicated arms. The young person should not look like a tiny child next to a giant adult.

Different Shoes, Shared Direction

An overhead view of two pairs of shoes pointing toward the same opening in a curved pavement edge. One pair is expressive and youthful; the other quieter. Both have similar visual weight. Their long shadows overlap into one calm shape.

being alongside someone, expressed through an ordinary moment.

Art direction

Colour coral trainers, deep teal shoes, a small sage ground fragment and apricot shadow.

Make it work show just enough of the legs to establish the viewpoint. Avoid a busy street scene. The shadows are the only secondary idea.

Space To Unfold

A folded coral paper form opens into a broad peach surface. A small figure stands comfortably at the fold, with generous room ahead. The folds suggest developing possibilities rather than a staircase to success.

growth can reveal space that was already there.

Art direction

Colour coral, peach and deep plum on Paper.

Make it work three or four folds maximum. It should still read as paper, not an impossible architectural puzzle.

A Bench With Room

A short curved bench viewed from above. One person sits at one end, turned slightly toward an intentionally empty seat. The bench and the open seat create a welcoming crescent of white space. A small bag rests on the ground, leaving the seat available.

availability and room for another person.

Art direction

Colour sage bench, deep plum figure, coral bag.

Make it work posture needs to feel relaxed and welcoming rather than lonely. This is the quietest, least literal option.

The Book That Becomes A Bridge

An open book viewed low enough that its lifted page blocks form a gentle arch. The same object reads as both an open book and a short bridge. A single tiny person crosses its top edge. A clean white opening beneath makes the bridge clear; a visible spine and a few page edges preserve the book reading.

research becomes a practical crossing.

Art direction

Colour deep teal cover, apricot pages, coral figure.

Make it work an approachable little crossing, not a dangerous bridge over a canyon. No extra landscape or flying pages.

Knowledge That Fits In A Pocket

A small open book tucked into an oversized jacket pocket. Its bookmark continues outside the pocket as a short path. Show only the pocket and a cropped section of garment, with no face or full body.

research people can carry becomes tangible and playful.

Art direction

Colour sage fabric, plum pocket edge, peach book and teal bookmark.

Make it work one pocket, one book, one bookmark. Avoid turning it into a collection of stationery.

The Gap Makes Sense

Two short stacks of rounded page-like shapes face one another. Their inner edges form the Paper-white silhouette of a key. Never draw the key itself. Give it a large simple head and one broad tooth.

useful understanding emerges between pieces of information.

Art direction

Colour plum and coral stacks with a pale apricot page edge.

Make it work this is a harder generation challenge. Begin with two flat silhouettes and simplify until the hidden key reads. Avoid suggesting that one tool solves everything.

A Book You Can Step Into

An open book viewed from above becomes a small landscape: its central gutter is a pale river, a broad page contour becomes a path, and a bookmark forms a crossing. The paper remains visibly paper.

knowledge becomes somewhere you can orient yourself.

Art direction

Colour Paper pages, sage contour, teal river and coral bookmark.

Make it work three landscape shapes only. Resist adding buildings, forests and several people. It should still be a book at first glance.

The Home Between The Branches

Two broad organic branches lean toward one another. Their inner contours leave a small house-shaped opening in Paper white: sloping roof, upright sides and flat base. Five simple leaves sit around the outside. The house is never drawn or outlined; it exists only as the gap.

family and belonging are held within the living structure.

Art direction

Colour evergreen branches, sage leaves and one coral accent.

Make it work do not add windows, a door or a second house on top. The house is an emotional metaphor for belonging, not a claim about a particular home.

Personal rootsconnection

Five Threads, One Everyday Object

Five broad soft ribbons arrive from different directions and join into the handle of a simple cup. Their colours remain separate near the edges, then resolve into one comfortable everyday form. Just one or two overlaps, not complicated weaving.

relationships and experiences become part of everyday identity.

Art direction

Colour sage, coral, peach, teal and plum, with a mostly Paper-white cup.

Make it work keep the handle believable. Five is a quiet family reference. Do not assign colours to ethnic backgrounds or invent a cultural weaving pattern.

A Window With Deep Roots

Look outward through a rounded window. Its lower frame continues down into a few root shapes. The view beyond consists of two soft land contours and one warm sky circle. The frame and roots form one continuous object.

where we stand shapes our view of the world.

Art direction

Colour plum frame and roots, sage land, apricot sun.

Make it work keep the landscape abstract rather than inventing a specific ancestral place. Use an isolated architectural fragment rather than a whole house scene.

The Roots Are The Empty Space

A compact tree canopy above a low oval of coloured earth. Broad white channels cut into the earth to form roots, continuing from the trunk. Above ground are five rounded leaf masses; below ground, support is shown through absence.

what supports someone is not always immediately visible.

Art direction

Colour sage canopy, evergreen trunk, coral earth and white roots.

Make it work broad graceful channels, not a dense botanical diagram. This is the simpler alternative if the hidden house in concept 9 proves difficult to generate.

The Pencil That Casts A Path

A short, substantial pencil lies diagonally across a white field. Its cast shadow unexpectedly becomes a broad curving path leading toward a peach semicircle. The shadow starts precisely at the pencil before opening into the route. Render everything as flat editorial shapes.

research and careful work become something useful to follow.

Art direction

Colour evergreen pencil, plum tip, sage shadow-path and peach destination.

Make it work one transformed shadow only. No extra arrows, graph, book or lighthouse. Keep other lighting cues minimal so the intentional shadow makes sense.

The Opening Is The Light

Two dark curved shapes form a simple lantern silhouette. Its white central opening continues outward as a widening beam. A peach shape near the beam's origin supplies warmth without a glowing effect.

clarity appears as space opening up.

Art direction

Colour deep teal lantern, coral handle accent, peach centre and white light.

Make it work a large continuous opening. Avoid a logo badge or a burst of radiating lines. The light should be part of the silhouette itself.

A Compass That Becomes A Table

Seen directly from above, a round meeting table has one elongated leaf-shaped object at its centre. Together they quietly suggest a compass face and needle. Three partial chair shapes establish the everyday scene. No compass letters or ticks.

direction emerges through shared work.

Art direction

Colour apricot tabletop, plum and teal chairs, sage leaf.

Make it work a subtle second reading, not a literal compass sitting on a table. This overlaps the counselling theme, so choose it if the wider set needs more human connection.

The work todaypractical tool and perspective

The Folded Map Makes Room

A folded map opens into a shallow three-dimensional form. Between two upright folds is a white doorway. A short route on the map ends at that opening. No labels, map pins or destination names.

practical tools help people find an opening and choose a next step.

Art direction

Colour sage and apricot map planes, teal route, coral fold edge.

Make it work the doorway is the actual gap between paper folds, not a separate door pasted onto a map. Keep the perspective gentle and physically readable.

Youth workPrompt variation A

Fresh core concept

Design a fresh illustration of a young sprout with a simple upward-growing curved stem, three generous leaves at alternating heights, tiny ground-level shoots, and small light accent above the highest leaf. Make the leaves soft filled shapes, not pale shapes enclosed by fine blue wire outlines. A single continuous slender stem holds the composition together. A very short, subtle grounding mark is enough; do not draw a long baseline across the whole canvas.

Warm sun variation

A young plant with three full, softly curved leaves and a gently bending stem, emerging from a small low earth shape. Behind only its upper half, place two or three offset warm sun arcs suggesting gentle sunlight through layered flat colour. Keep these arcs partial, airy and much smaller than a full background disk. The sprout remains the clear focal point. Use soft natural asymmetry; no perfectly mirrored leaves.

Fresh core concept

Design a fresh illustration of an open book, viewed almost straight on with a gentle suggestion of depth. Give it a central spine and two gently curved page spreads. Make the book feel light, accessible and inviting. On the left page use three short abstract line marks, not readable writing; on the right use two soft wave curves and a small warm circle. The marks should be large enough to read as simple shapes at thumbnail size. Use a slightly visible cover and layered page edges, not a detailed stack of individual sheets.

Personal rootsPrompt variation A

Fresh core concept

Design a fresh illustration of an upright plant in a simple tapered pot, with exactly five broad leaves and a branching stem. The pot is a clean flat cutaway shape, with a small, simplified root system visible inside it: one main root and two or three side roots. Treat this as a gentle editorial metaphor, not a botanical diagram or a transparent glass pot. The branches and leaves should be softly asymmetrical and clearly separate. No long baseline.

Warm ceramic pot

A rounded, low ceramic pot holding a balanced but naturally asymmetrical plant with exactly five generous leaves. Use simple interlocking organic shapes and one clear stem. In the pot's front face, show a quiet abstract root motif made from three connected, flowing strokes; it should read as a visual metaphor for roots, not surface ornament or a cultural pattern. The work todayPrompt variation C

Minimal guiding light

A highly simplified lighthouse made from four or five carefully balanced filled shapes: a soft tapered tower, small lantern, shallow roof, tiny arched doorway and a restrained curved base. The tower is slightly off-centre left, leaving generous space for one warm beam to the right. Add only two short curved water marks at the base. Give the silhouette gentle asymmetry and soften sharp corners without making it toy-like. This is the most minimal option in the set.
