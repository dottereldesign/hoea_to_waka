Current Website Audit

Problems, risks, useful carryovers, and rewrite implications based on the current site.

Overall Assessment

The current site contains meaningful material, but it does not present the business with enough clarity, precision, or credibility.

The biggest issue is not lack of substance. The issue is that the substance is poorly organised and weakly expressed.

Main Problems

1. Positioning is too soft and too broad

The current site tries to speak to organisations, leaders, support professionals, individuals, teachers, whanau, and book buyers.

Result: first-time visitors work too hard to understand whether the offer is for them, and the commercial offer feels less focused than it probably is.

2. The homepage does not quickly answer buyer questions

A professional visitor likely wants to know what Hoea to Waka is, who it is for, what outcomes it helps create, what formats are available, why Anna is credible, and what to do next.

The current homepage touches these points, but not with enough directness or hierarchy.

3. Copy quality reduces trust

The live site contains spelling issues, spacing issues, grammar errors, repeated ideas, and inconsistent formatting.

This matters because the audience includes professional and institutional buyers. Weak copy can make a credible offer look less established than it is.

4. Service separation is unclear

There are three main service areas on the current site, but they are not sharply differentiated.

A better rebuild should clarify in-house workshops for organisations, one-on-one support or coaching, and specialist training for support professionals.

5. The site explains the model more than it sells the offer

There is substantial language about wellbeing, culture, reflection, and resilience.

What is less clear is buying process, workshop outcomes, delivery options, duration, who should enquire, and what happens after contact.

6. Proof is weak or underused

The current site shows one testimonial fragment, founder background, and books/resources, but does not strongly leverage testimonials, case studies, client logos, delivery history, outcomes, or credentials in context.

7. Information architecture is too flat

The navigation exists, but the site still feels like a collection of text blocks rather than a deliberate funnel.

There is no clear narrative path such as:

  1. Understand the offer
  2. See who it is for
  3. Build trust
  4. Choose a service
  5. Make contact

What Should Be Preserved

  • Strong cultural grounding in Aotearoa
  • The Hoea to Waka metaphor and identity
  • Anna's lived and professional credibility
  • The connection between resilience, reflection, and practical application
  • The books and supporting resources as trust-building assets
  • The kayak / wahine imagery if it is already recognised in Anna's other materials

Rewrite Implications

  • A much sharper homepage with one primary audience in mind
  • Clearer service pages built around outcomes, not abstract explanation
  • A stronger founder section tied to trust and relevance
  • Simpler language without losing cultural depth
  • Cleaner calls to action
  • A better distinction between core services and secondary resources

Suggested Messaging Priorities

  1. What Hoea to Waka does
  2. Who it helps
  3. Why it is credible
  4. What services are available
  5. What makes it distinct in Aotearoa
  6. How to enquire

Risks if Rebuilt Poorly

  • Overexplaining the model again without clarifying the offer
  • Making the site feel too clinical and losing warmth
  • Making the site feel too soft and losing commercial clarity
  • Treating books/resources as the main offer when they may be secondary
  • Writing for everyone instead of prioritising the actual buyer

Practical Direction

The best outcome is probably a site that feels:

  • Calm
  • Credible
  • Culturally grounded
  • Clear to institutional buyers
  • Human rather than corporate
  • Professionally written
