Brand Overview

The brand

Brand Overview

Hoea tō Waka provides resilience training, individual coaching and learning resources for organisations and the people who support others. The work is grounded in Aotearoa’s cultural context. Explain what the model helps people do before describing how it looks.

Brand name

Hoea tō Waka

Write the name as three words: Hoea tō Waka. Keep the line above the ō in tō; this is a macron. Use this spelling in headings, documents and social posts.

Brand line

Strength in Aotearoa

This short line sits with the name in the main logo. Keep the wording together when using that version of the logo.

Vision

To see stronger, more resilient people, teams, and communities across Aotearoa.

The vision describes the future the work aims to contribute to.

Mission

To deliver practical, culturally grounded resilience training, coaching, and resources that help people move through challenge with clarity and direction.

The mission describes what Hoea tō Waka does and how it supports people.
02

Audience

Our readers

Who we speak to

The same person may be booking training for a team, looking for their own support or helping someone else. Write for what they need to decide, rather than assuming they know the model.

People arranging training

HR staff, people leaders and managers need to know who a workshop is for, what it covers and how to enquire. Explain the format and how it can be tailored to their team.

Leaders and team members

Someone considering individual coaching needs a clear picture of the conversation and the next step. Describe the practical focus without making them feel they should already have the answers.

Support professionals

People working with clients, whānau and communities need to understand how the model can fit their practice. Use examples from their working context and explain specialist terms.

People using resources

Readers, whānau, educators and workshop participants need to know what a book or video is about and how to get it. Make the title, purpose and order or viewing link easy to find.

Typography

Type system

Typography

Typography means the fonts and text styles we use. Inter is the main font for the Hoea tō Waka name, headings, paragraphs, menus and buttons. Use a heavier weight for headings so readers can quickly see what matters.

ABC Marist Book is the serif font used for testimonial quotes. Its letter shapes give people’s words a distinct voice. Keep the quote heading, person’s name and controls in Inter. The four examples show these roles using text from the site.

Regular 400, Semibold 600 and Book 350 name the font weights: how light or heavy the letters look. A serif font has small finishing strokes on its letters; Inter has simpler ends. Compare the samples to see the difference.

Keep it consistent

Use sentence case: capitalise the first word and proper names. Website paragraphs are 1.1rem, or 17.6px at the default text size. Let longer text wrap instead of shrinking it to fit. Keep the macrons in Hoea tō Waka, whānau and tīpuna.

Section Spacing

Layout rhythm

Section Spacing

Section spacing is the empty space above and below a block of content. It separates one topic from the next and stops the page feeling crowded. The current website uses the values below for standard sections; smaller screens use less space.

Desktop 92px
Tablet 65px
Mobile 60px

How to apply it

For a standard website section, use 92px above and below on screens wider than 1100px, 65px from 761px to 1100px, and 60px at 760px or below. If two connected blocks need to sit together, remove the spacing on the joining edge instead of adding a smaller, unrelated value. Page headers and the inset chapter banners keep their own layout spacing.

Can people read the text?

Contrast is the difference between the text and its background. A higher ratio means a stronger difference. Aim for at least 4.5:1 for body text and small labels. Large text can use 3:1: at least 24px in regular weight or about 18.7px in bold. The table checks the stricter body-text standard and updates when you edit a colour.

Using and updating this guide

Use the examples to make consistent decisions, then check the result in the place people will see it: a phone, browser, printed card or workshop handout. Keep the name, logo and font roles consistent; let the amount of content determine the layout. When a brand decision changes, update this guide and its examples together.

For brand wording or an application not covered here, contact Anna with the draft and where it will be used.
