Brand Overview

The brand

Brand Overview

Hoea tō Waka provides resilience training, individual coaching and learning resources for organisations and the people who support others. The work is grounded in Aotearoa’s cultural context. Explain what the model helps people do before describing how it looks.

Brand name

Hoea tō Waka

Brand line

Strength in Aotearoa

Vision

To see stronger, more resilient people, teams, and communities across Aotearoa.

Mission

To deliver practical, culturally grounded resilience training, coaching, and resources that help people move through challenge with clarity and direction.

02

Audience

Our readers

Who we speak to

The same person may be booking training for a team, looking for their own support or helping someone else. Write for what they need to decide, rather than assuming they know the model.

People arranging training

HR staff, people leaders and managers need to know who a workshop is for, what it covers and how to enquire. Explain the format and how it can be tailored to their team.

Leaders and team members

Someone considering individual coaching needs a clear picture of the conversation and the next step. Describe the practical focus without making them feel they should already have the answers.

Support professionals

People working with clients, whānau and communities need to understand how the model can fit their practice. Use examples from their working context and explain specialist terms.

People using resources

Readers, whānau, educators and workshop participants need to know what a book or video is about and how to get it. Make the title, purpose and order or viewing link easy to find.

Typography

Type system

Typography

Typography means the fonts and text styles we use. Inter is the main font for the Hoea tō Waka name, headings, paragraphs, menus and buttons. Use a heavier weight for headings so readers can quickly see what matters.

Inter is our single typeface for now, including testimonials, labels and previews. Create hierarchy through size, spacing and weight rather than a second font. The specimens and usage guide show these roles using text from the site.

Regular 400 and Semibold 600 name the font weights: how light or heavy the letters look. Use Regular for paragraphs and quotes, and Semibold for headings and actions.

Download Font packInter 4.1: four static TTF styles, upright and italic variable TTFs, and two web WOFF2 files. Licence and installation notes included.

For office and design apps, choose Desktop/Static for Regular 400, Semibold 600 and their true italics. Choose Desktop/Variable instead if your app supports variable fonts; Web contains the WOFF2 versions. Use italics for short emphasis within text, and keep logo lettering upright. Use Inter Regular 400 for testimonial quotes.

Keep it consistent

Use sentence case: capitalise the first word and proper names. Website paragraphs are 1.1rem, or 17.6px at the default text size. Let longer text wrap instead of shrinking it to fit. Keep the macrons in Hoea tō Waka, whānau and tīpuna.

UI Buttons & Icons

Interface essentials

UI Buttons & Icons

Clear buttons and familiar symbols help people find their way around the website. Use a consistent outline style for familiar actions, with a short text label whenever the meaning could be unclear.

These are interface symbols. Use the supplied Hoea tō Waka artwork for the brand logo and favicon.

One clear visual language

Shape & stroke

Build on a 24 × 24 viewBox with no fill, rounded caps and rounded joins. Use a light, consistent outline; the examples use a 1.6-unit stroke.

Size & spacing

Use 18px for compact actions and 24px where more emphasis is needed. Keep the proportions intact and centre the artwork within its control. Small artwork still needs a generous hit area: aim for at least 44 × 44px for touch controls.

Colour & state

Let icons inherit the text colour. Use dark marks on light surfaces and light marks on dark surfaces, with enough contrast to stay visible. Pair success or error colours with a symbol and words.

Labels & meaning

Keep the same symbol for the same action. Hide decorative icons from screen readers; give an icon-only button a clear accessible name. Label unfamiliar actions in visible text rather than relying on hover.

Small artwork, comfortable controls

Size examples are static illustrations. In a working interface, the whole control should be clickable and have a visible keyboard focus state.

Buttons: make the next step clear

The main website action is a rounded Ink button with white text and a white arrow circle. On a dark background, reverse it to white with dark text. Use a quieter outlined button for an alternative action. Labels should describe the action, such as “Explore services” or “Talk with Anna”.

Section Spacing

Use a consistent rhythm between related content, cards and whole sections. These are frequently used spacing ranges across the website. Identical fluid formulas share one definition; component-specific exceptions remain separate.

Above and below standard sections.

Standard section spacing

Use var(--section-space) above and below standard sections. It grows smoothly from 60px at a 390px viewport to 92px at 1440px, staying within those limits. At 768px it is about 72px; at 1100px it is about 82px.

Pixel examples assume a 16px root size. The rem limits respect the reader’s font settings.

How to apply it

Keep related elements close and leave more space between topics. Reuse the existing shared spacing for a component before introducing another size. If two connected blocks sit together, remove spacing on the joining edge.

Keep the 8px page inset, tiny alignment gaps and negative offsets where needed. Navigation clearance, animation layouts, game controls and logo construction have their own requirements.

Developer spacing reference 214 individual definitions

Individual values are counted once, rather than counting every combination of top, right, bottom and left padding as a new size. It does not claim every rule is visible in every state.

Before you publish

Check readability on a phone, confirm dates and links, and get permission for photographs. Add alternative text to images, captions to video, and key information in the post caption.

Using and updating this guide

Use the examples to make consistent decisions, then check the result in the place people will see it: a phone, browser, printed card or workshop handout. That is a judgement about the current scope and execution, not a measured market ranking. There is no evidence here to claim “the best in NZ or Australia”. Animation alone does not justify a premium: the stronger case is helping partners find the right assets and use them correctly.

A higher enterprise price would need a broader brief: validated strategy, more production and co-branding rules, client usability evidence, formal accessibility review and documented governance. The current page is maintained through code and deployment; a client-editable CMS, approval workflows and asset access controls are not included.

Why NZ$135/hour? It sits between Noko’s NZ$130/hour for smaller jobs and Seed Studio’s NZ$150/hour web development rate. Seed also lists NZ$80/hour for branding/design. The blended rate is a modelling choice, not a published industry average.

How the range is derived: 100 hours × NZ$120 × 1.10 = NZ$13,200 at the lower end; 145 hours × NZ$150 × 1.10 = NZ$23,925 at the upper end. Rounded to NZ$13,000–24,000, excluding GST. Content readiness, bespoke animation, review rounds and reuse of existing systems determine where a project lands.

Scope boundaries: This covers a comparable guide using the existing brand, not a new identity or the whole marketing website. Naming, research-led positioning, original logo design, cultural consultation, print production, font licences, domain/hosting costs, CMS development and ongoing maintenance need separate scopes. Live updates still require someone to edit, test and publish them.

Keeping it current: An illustrative support allowance of 1–3 hours a month at NZ$135/hour would be NZ$135–405 + GST, only if that amount of work is needed. This is a budgeting example, not an offered subscription.
